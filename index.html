About

My interest in data started with soccer. When a player lost the ball on a risky pass, I wanted to know whether it was a bad decision or a reasonable gamble, and passing accuracy couldn't tell me. Since then I've paid attention to what a number leaves out, and that question runs through most of my research.

I like taking things apart. It started with a 30-year-old standing clock at my grandparents' house, and these days I can fix most things. I'm a railfan, I like long road trips, and my cameras have come with me to ten countries on four continents. I build Lego, play board games like Avalon with friends, and will happily try the same dish at three restaurants to compare.

Research

Content Hidden Behind Execution

2025 – 2026

Second author, with Prof. Jialu Zhang, University of Waterloo

A safety audit of 500 public Scratch projects. In 467 of them, you had to run or interact with the project before its main safety-relevant signal showed up, because the metadata alone wasn't enough. I screened projects, removed duplicates, reviewed AI labels and flagged cases. Submitted to AAAI-27.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03700

One Pixel Short

2025 – 2026

First author & experimental lead

Why the same Scratch program with the same inputs can behave differently across browsers and display settings. I tested 473 games in 3 browsers, traced the differences in collision detection back to pixel rendering, and designed and checked fixes. Wrote a 12-page paper with a co-author. Now in Macao's selection round for ISEF.

ScratchWorld

2025 – 2026

Independent research, first author

Can AI models predict what changes in a program after an action? I built 892 Scratch-based tests, wrote replay and scoring tools, and evaluated 7 models. Some models scored 91.4% accuracy just by copying the starting state, so I designed a value-aware changed-field F1 metric that checks whether they got the actual changes right.
